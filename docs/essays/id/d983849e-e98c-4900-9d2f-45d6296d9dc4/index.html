Music, Fashion, Film

Artist: Charli XCX

Date: 2026-07-24

Genre: pop

300 Words By Jihoo Kim

Punk rock died half a century ago. The spirit of rebellion, and the stylishness of the concept remains, but the industry seems hopeless in connecting the two since then. Although the separation of music and reality allowed music to thrive, as for it to be enjoyed without guilt, it also invalidated its message. Now Charli XCX must bear the realization, “nothing's gonna save us, not music, fashion or film.”

Within Music, Fashion, Film the punk rock influences are clear. The rejection of traditional choruses, minimalist arrangement and strong distorted guitars would easily fit as a modern variation of Killing Joke or The Jesus Lizard. Compare it with Charli XCX’s brand of icons, the commercially successful, the artsy but not classified as punk, John Cale, Marc Jacobs, Martin Scorsese and David Cronenberg.

Music, Fashion, Film is bombarded with Charli XCX’s personality. She repeats: she has changed, is not the rebellious kid anymore - shopping for modest clothes and a little bit more careful when jumping on trampolines - and learned to be more straightforward with her feelings. It is clear that she, like many other popular musicians, must live a reversed notion of an artist. The audience finds relevancy in her persona rather than her music.

During the struggle with her persona and herself, Charli XCX confronts the fragility of the persona. Subsequently, Music, Fashion, Film becomes a metacommentary on itself. With the death of the persona and the message, she acknowledges the death of music, then of the album itself. This plague is oddly meditating. As human mortality integrates art, she discovers a different approach to her artistry.
